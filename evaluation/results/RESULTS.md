# Evaluation results

Generated by `python -m evaluation.run` on `cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit`.

- Test split: 1759 resume-job pairs (857 No Fit, 444 Potential Fit, 458 Good Fit) across 71 jobs, 28 of which have both Good Fit and No Fit candidates and so count towards the within-job AUC.
- Training split: 6241 pairs across 280 jobs. No test job appears in training; 476 of the 477 test resumes do.
- Embedding model: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`.

AUC is the chance that a Good Fit pair outscores a No Fit pair: 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect. Brackets are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (jobs are resampled for the within-job AUC, pairs for the other two).

## Unseen-resume protocol

476 of the 477 test resumes also appear in the training split, paired with other jobs. The leakage check in the first table shows why that matters: ranking candidates purely by how their resume was labelled in training, without reading the job, already scores well. A trained model can therefore gain from remembering resumes, which would not carry over to new candidates.

Here resumes are divided into 4 folds, and each test pair is scored by a model trained without any resume from that pair's fold, so the model has seen neither the job nor the resume. Candidates are compared only with others in the same job and fold (84 such groups have both Good Fit and No Fit candidates), so these numbers are not directly comparable with the first table. Methods that need no training are unaffected by the protocol and are re-measured on the same groups.
